Missed weeks of training? Get your plan back on track

General information, not medical or coaching advice. Return-to-running wording reviewed 18 November 2026; the dated simulation below has not been rerun.

Illness, travel, a bad month at work. Most plans are written for the runner who never misses a week. Here is how to decide what to do, and what Paceline does.

First: why did you miss training?

The length of the gap is only part of the decision. Illness, injury and a busy week are different situations. The options below describe the app, not a return-to-running prescription.

  • A few days or one week: do not cram in missed runs. Before resuming, review the next run's distance and effort against what you can manage now, rather than following the calendar alone.
  • Two to four weeks, with 12 or more weeks to a marathon: Paceline may offer to rebuild from today to the same race day. The plan gets shorter and the build is compressed; review it before accepting.
  • A gap that leaves too few weeks: a rebuild may not be available. Skipping the missed runs clears them from the calendar; it does not establish that you are ready for the next workout or the race.

What Paceline offers

Paceline can rebuild your plan from today and keep your race day. It shows a review screen first, and tells you when long runs restart lower than they were. If a rebuild is not possible, it offers to skip the missed runs and carry on.

Skipping missed runs does not reduce every upcoming workout. Check the next session's distance and intensity rather than assuming that clearing the misses has made the remaining schedule suitable.

Things to know

  • The rebuild restarts from your baseline, not from where you had got to. A runner who had built to 25 km long runs restarts at about 13 km.
  • These numbers are from one simulated runner, not an average. They can change when the plan generator changes.
  • The test did not cover ultras, or 2-, 3-, 5- or 6-day weeks.

If you are ill or injured

Paceline has a recovery break, separate from the rebuild, that can ease planned training. Neither option is medical clearance. After illness or injury, seek advice from a qualified health professional about when and how to return, especially if symptoms persist or you are unsure. A race date is not a reason to override that advice.

Further reading: the IOC consensus on respiratory illness and return to sport describes an individual, graded return based on illness severity and response to activity, not just days missed. POGO Physio's guide to interrupted training discusses why both volume and intensity need review.

Common questions

Should I add the missed miles back in? No. Drop them rather than squeeze them in.
What if I am under 12 weeks from a marathon? A rebuild is not offered. Paceline offers to skip the missed runs, but that does not automatically reduce the remaining sessions or confirm race readiness.
Does it cost anything? The whole plan is free on Android, including the rebuild and the recovery break.
