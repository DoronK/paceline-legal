Tempo run vs interval run: what is the difference?

Sources checked 23 November 2026. General training information, not an individual training or medical prescription.

A tempo run usually means a sustained, controlled effort that is harder than easy running. An interval session breaks running into repeated work periods with recovery between them. The terms overlap: a tempo workout can also be split into intervals.

Read the target effort and recovery instructions, not just the workout name. A title alone cannot tell you how fast to run.

Tempo and intervals at a glance

Question Tempo running Interval running
What does the label usually emphasize? Sustaining a controlled effort Repeating work periods with recovery
Is it always continuous? No. Tempo intervals break it into sections No continuous main effort is implied; read the recovery steps
Is it a sprint? Not by default Not by default
What should I check? Target effort or pace, duration and whether breaks are included Repetitions, work duration or distance, target effort, and recovery duration and type

Names vary between coaches and plans. Some use tempo for threshold running; others use it more broadly for sustained work, including race-pace efforts. Follow the definition and instructions in the plan you are using.

What should a tempo run feel like?

Tempo work is often described as controlled or "comfortably hard": more demanding than an easy conversation-paced run, without turning the session into a race. A coaching description may use short sentences or phrases as a rough effort cue.

That is a description, not a personal pace calculation. Do not copy someone else's speed or a generic watch-zone number and assume it is your tempo target. Read your own session's guidance.

If you are still learning easy effort, start with the easy-run guide rather than adding tempo work because its name sounds like the next level.

What makes a run an interval session?

Intervals divide the main work into repeatable sections. The recovery might be a jog, a walk or a rest, depending on the instructions.

An interval label does not automatically mean all-out speed. Tempo intervals and cruise intervals use breaks too. The purpose of a session depends on its effort, work duration and recovery together.

Before starting, check:

  • How many work periods are there?
  • Is each one measured in time or distance?
  • What pace or effort is prescribed?
  • How long is the recovery, and what should you do during it?
  • Where do the warm-up and cool-down fit?

If the recovery is missing or unclear, check the full session or ask your coach. Do not borrow the recovery from another workout with a similar title.

Why do tempo intervals appear in both categories?

The words describe different parts of the workout. Tempo can describe the intended effort; intervals can describe how the work is divided.

A continuous tempo section and several tempo sections separated by recovery are not identical sessions. Do not remove the breaks, run each section faster, or add repetitions just to make one resemble the other.

Which one should I choose?

Neither label is automatically better. Choose a plan that fits your current running, available days and goal, then follow its session progression. Do not add a second demanding session simply to include both types in one week.
