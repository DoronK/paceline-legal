Before choosing a sample

Start with the running you do now, not only the race you want to finish. A beginner label does not make every plan a suitable starting point.

  • Choose running days that fit your week before comparing the sample schedules.
  • Understand easy effort before chasing a pace when you read the workout targets.
  • Check what you need without a smartwatch, including the difference between a plan and a run tracker.

Then compare a sample's stated inputs and first week with your current training.

How to read a workout in the table

Each cell is a workout title, not the full session instructions. A repetition label tells you about repeated work; it does not by itself describe the warm-up, recovery between efforts or cool-down.

  • Read the units. Metres or kilometres describe distance; minutes describe time. A repeat count tells you how many times to do the stated work, not how long the whole outing takes.
  • Look beyond the main set. In these examples, an interval or tempo title can describe only the work portion. The full workout may include separate warm-up, recovery and cool-down. Do not add the title numbers and assume they are your complete weekly distance.
  • Open the full workout before running. In Paceline, read the session's steps, recoveries and pace or effort guidance. If an instruction is unclear, check it rather than guess or fill in a recovery from a different plan.

These tables help you compare sample plans. They are not a personal workout prescription. Start with your current running and the plan's starting requirements, not a session copied from an advanced schedule.

How the ultra plans work

50K20 weeks14 weeks3 weeks
50 mile24 weeks16 weeks3 weeks
100K24 weeks16 weeks3 weeks
  • Back-to-back long runs. In the 50-mile and 100K plans, each build-week long run is paired with a second long run the next day, to practise running on tired legs.
  • Finish-focused 50 mile and 100K. The 50-mile and 100K plans have no goal time: terrain and elevation make a flat goal pace meaningless. The 50K plan takes a goal time.
  • Who they are for. The 50-mile and 100K are offered to intermediate and advanced runners.
  • When your plan changes. In the app, Paceline can trim an upcoming long run when at least two of three recovery signals (HRV, resting heart rate, time in bed) point to fatigue. It never adds volume and nothing changes until you confirm. Recovery signals need Health Connect, which is optional.

What Paceline does not do

  • It does not record runs or guide you by voice. Use any tracker you like.
  • It does not push workouts to a watch.
  • It does not know your course. Terrain, elevation and aid-station timing are yours to plan for.
