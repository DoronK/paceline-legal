A free Runna alternative that adapts to your recovery

Checked on 10 November 2026. Prices and limits change; if something here is out of date, tell us.

Runna earned its reputation: it's polished, it syncs workouts to your watch, it has strength plans and a big coaching library. If you want to be guided through every run on your wrist and you're happy paying for it, Runna is genuinely good and you should probably just pay for it. This page is for the runners who searched for a Runna alternative because the subscription is the problem.

The short version: Paceline is a free Android app whose whole training plan, every distance, every week, is free with no trial countdown. The plan adapts to how recovered you are, explains every change, and waits for your approval. Runna Premium is $19.99 a month or $119.99 a year after a 7-day trial.

Where Paceline is different

The full adaptive plan is free. No trial countdown. Every distance from 5K to 100K, every week, adaptation, notifications and fueling guidance. There is no timer running out on week one and no card on file. Pro is optional ($49.99 a year or $6.99 a month) and adds extras like a race-week brief and progress trends; the plan itself never sits behind it.
It adapts to recovery, not just pace. Connect Health Connect and Paceline reads HRV, resting heart rate and time in bed. When at least two of the three point to fatigue, it proposes trimming an upcoming long run. It never adds volume on its own, and Runna says heart-rate data “will not influence your training plan”.
Every change is explained and approved by you. When the plan changes, Paceline says what changed and why before it happens, and you can undo it. Your plan never silently shifts under you.
No watch required. Paceline lives on your Android phone as a planning layer. Record runs with whatever you already use; you don't need a Garmin, an Apple Watch or anything else on your wrist to get the full plan.

Where Runna is better

Honesty beats a sales pitch, so: Runna has an iOS app (Paceline is Android only), watch apps that push workouts to Garmin, Apple Watch, COROS, Suunto and Amazfit, strength and mobility programmes, in-run audio coaching, and years of track record from a large team. Paceline is built by one developer and is newer. If those matter more to you than the price, Runna is the right call. For the full feature-by-feature breakdown, see Paceline vs Runna.

Common questions

Is Paceline really free?

Yes. Every distance, every week of the plan, the adaptation engine, notifications and fueling guidance are free, with no trial countdown. An optional Pro subscription ($49.99 a year or $6.99 a month) adds a race-week brief, a recovery view, a periodization override and progress trends.

Does it work without a Garmin or Apple Watch?

Yes. Paceline is a planning layer on your Android phone, not a watch app. Recovery signals come from Health Connect (HRV, resting heart rate, time in bed), and you keep using whatever you already use to record the run itself.

Can it adjust my plan when I'm sick?

Yes. A built-in not-feeling-100% mode lightens your load and ramps you back, and recovery-based trims propose shortening an upcoming long run when at least two of HRV, resting heart rate and sleep point to fatigue. Every change is explained, needs your approval, and can be undone.
