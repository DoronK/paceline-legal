Comparing running-plan apps? Start with what you need.

Last reviewed 18 November 2026. Apps change; if something here is out of date, tell us.

This is for runners comparing running-plan apps. It covers what Paceline does differently from other apps, and the things it does not do, so you can decide before you install.

The short version: Paceline is a free, planning-only Android app. It builds a full plan for a race from 5K to 100K and proposes long-run trims from your training and optional recovery data. You review those changes. It does not record your runs, push workouts to a watch or coach you by voice. If those are essential, look for an app that explicitly supports them.

Free or paid: compare the job, not just the price

A free plan, a free tier and a free trial are different offers. Before paying, check whether the running plan itself is included, whether a trial renews into a subscription, and whether extra hardware is required. Paying does not by itself tell you whether a plan fits your running.

Use the same questions for any app, including Paceline:

  • Does the starting point match what you run now? Look at the first week, the longest session and the rest days, not only the race distance or a beginner label. A couch-to-5K programme and a half marathon plan can have very different starting points. Do not compress a plan just to fit a race date.
  • Can the week fit your life? Check the running days, the time for a long run, and what happens after a missed workout. Moving a session is different from rebuilding a plan after a break.
  • Do you need a plan, a recorder or in-run guidance? A calendar of workouts does not automatically include GPS recording, audio cues or workouts sent to your watch. Check your exact phone and watch, not just a general integration logo.
  • What does adaptive mean here? Ask what data it uses, what it changes, and whether you can review or undo a change. Do not assume every free plan is fixed, or that every paid app changes your plan in the same way.
  • Do you want a person to coach you? An automated plan is not the same as access to a human coach. If individual advice matters, check who answers your questions and what support is actually included.
  • What remains free? Separate the whole plan from optional insights, a limited free tier or a trial. Read the current renewal price and cancellation terms before accepting a subscription.

You may prefer a written plan, an app with live guidance, a watch-based plan or a coach. There is no single feature list that makes one option right for every runner. For a starting-point example, the NHS Couch to 5K programme begins with run/walk sessions. For a longer-distance comparison, REI's half marathon guide explains the base expected before its plan.

Where Paceline fits

The whole plan is free. Every distance, every week, adjustments, reminders and fueling guidance. Some other running-plan apps charge a subscription for the full plan. Pro is optional and adds a race-week brief, a recovery view, a periodization override and progress trends. Check the purchase screen for current local pricing and any trial terms before upgrading. You do not need Pro for the whole training plan.
It can read recovery, not only pace. If you connect Health Connect, Paceline looks at HRV, resting heart rate and time in bed, and proposes trimming an upcoming long run when at least two of the three point to fatigue. It never adds volume and never changes anything until you confirm. Health Connect is optional; without it the plan adjusts from your logged workouts.
Move a workout to any day. You can move a workout to any date from today up to the day before your race. Paceline warns about back-to-back hard days or a sudden long-run jump but does not block you.
Distances up to 100K. Plans for 5K, 10K, half marathon, marathon, 50K, 50 mile and 100K. The 50 mile and 100K are offered to intermediate and advanced runners.
Fueling with numbers. Per-hour carb, fluid and sodium targets and fueling rehearsals on your long runs.

What Paceline does not do

  • It does not record runs. It is a planning layer. Pair it with the tracker or watch you already use.
  • It does not push workouts to a watch.
  • No voice coaching or audio cues while you run.
  • Android only. There is no iOS app yet.
  • English only for now.
  • It needs an account and an internet connection to build a plan. Once built, the plan is saved on your device and backed up to your account.
  • It is newer and smaller. It is built by one developer, so expect fewer extras than the big apps.

Who it fits

  • You want a full race plan without a subscription.
  • You are on Android and already track runs elsewhere.
  • You train for ultras, or you want a plan that can respond to recovery data.

If you want workouts sent to your watch or voice guidance in one app, check those features explicitly. Paceline is not an all-in-one option for that job.
